The problem

Most people can’t see a rip until they’re in one

A rip is a narrow channel of water flowing out through the surf. It doesn’t splash or roar. From the sand it often looks like the calmest gap between the waves, and that is where people walk in.

How it works

From a public camera to a warning on your phone

No new hardware. RipCo uses the camera that already watches the beach and a model trained to read the surface of the surf.

  1. A camera that already exists

    Many beaches have a public camera pointed at the water. RipCo reads that feed. Nothing new is mounted or maintained.

  2. The model reads the surf

    Frame by frame, it looks for a darker channel, a gap in the line of breaking waves and foam moving out to sea.

  3. A warning you can read at a glance

    A likely rip is outlined in red with the model’s confidence beside it, and the reminder to check the flags stays on screen.

In the app

Rip detection, and the tools around it

Detection is the reason RipCo exists. Around it sit the conditions, a risk score, alerts, live cameras and two community tools, so one beach can be checked in one place.

The core feature

Live camera with rip detection

The live view from the beach camera, with likely rips outlined in red and a confidence figure beside each one. I trained and tested the model on a public camera at Manly Beach; other beaches with a camera stream live without detection.

Red outline
Where the model sees the signature of a rip in the current frame.
Confidence
How sure the model is, as a percentage.
Risk score
Each beach card also carries a score out of 100 built from the live conditions and labelled as a level of risk. How the score is built.

Safety brief

A short plain-English summary of what the water is doing at your beach right now, with the flags reminder built in.

Conditions and risk score

Water and air temperature, wind and swell for your beach, and a risk score out of 100 built from them, shown as a level of risk.

Alerts

The app watches your saved beaches and flags changing conditions before you head out.

Live cameras

Beaches with a public camera stream live in the app and carry a Live tag. Detection runs on the stream only where it has been tested.

Shark Watch

Hazard sightings reported by other people at the beach, shared with everyone nearby. Reports are unverified.

Ask RipCo

A conversational assistant for conditions and beach-safety questions, built to point you to the flags and the lifesavers rather than give a verdict.

About the project, the research and the design decisions

A note from the maker

I surveyed beachgoers for this project and most of the people I asked could not confidently pick a rip from the sand. The cameras that could show them were already mounted and streaming. The missing piece was a way to read those feeds, so I built one.

RipCo is a prototype. I have trained detection on one public camera at Manly Beach, and the app is not on the App Store yet.
