The problem

Most people can’t see a rip until they’re in one

A rip is a narrow channel of water flowing out through the surf. It doesn’t splash or roar. From the sand it often looks like the calmest gap between the waves, and that is where people walk in.

How it works

From a public camera to a warning on your phone

No new hardware. RipCo uses the camera that already watches the beach and a model trained to read the surface of the surf.

  1. Existing public cameras

    Many beaches already have a public camera pointed at the water. RipCo reads that feed. There is nothing to install and nothing to mount.

  2. The model reads the surf

    Frame by frame, it looks for the signature of a rip: a darker channel, a gap in the line of breaking waves, foam moving out to sea.

  3. A warning you can read at a glance

    A likely rip is outlined in red with the model’s confidence. The app has no green state and never tells you the water is fine to enter.

In the app

Rip detection, and the tools around it

Detection is the reason RipCo exists. Four other tools sit beside it so you can check one beach in one place before you leave the house.

The core feature

Live camera with rip detection

The live view from the beach camera, with likely rips outlined in red and a confidence figure beside each one. Detection has been developed and tested on a public camera at Manly Beach. Every other beach in the app shows conditions only.

Red outline
Where the model sees the signature of a rip in the current frame.
Confidence
How sure the model is, as a percentage. A reading from a camera, not a guarantee.
No green state
The app never marks water as safe. Nothing found means nothing found.

Safety brief

A short plain-English summary of what the water is doing at your beach right now, with the flags reminder built in.

Marine conditions

Wind, swell, tide and water temperature for your beach in one view, so you know what to expect before you arrive.

Shark Watch

Hazard sightings reported by other people at the beach, shared with everyone nearby. Reports are unverified.

Ask RipCo

A conversational assistant for conditions and beach-safety questions. Its answers point you back to the flags and the lifeguards.

All five tools in detail

RipCo never tells you a beach is safe

The app shows what it detects and what the conditions are. It can miss a rip, a camera can go down, and the water changes in minutes. Whatever the screen says, swim between the red and yellow flags and follow the lifeguards. Read the full commitment.

About the project, the research and the design decisions

A note from the maker

I surveyed beachgoers for this project and most of the people I asked could not confidently pick a rip from the sand. The cameras that could show them were already mounted and streaming. The missing piece was a way to read those feeds, so I built one.

RipCo is a prototype. Detection has been developed on a public camera at Manly Beach and the app is not on the App Store yet. This site describes the app as it is today, including what it can’t do.
