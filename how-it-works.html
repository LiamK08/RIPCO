Reading the surface

The clues can
be surprisingly subtle.

These are some of the visual signs the model looks for. They can be hard to spot, and a rip will not always show every clue.

VISUAL CUE 01

A darker channel

Deeper water can look darker than the shallower water around it.

VISUAL CUE 02

A break in the waves

A gap in the breaking surf can be a clue. Smoother-looking water is not an all-clear.

VISUAL CUE 03

Water moving seaward

Foam, sand or debris moving away from shore can reveal the direction of flow.

Learn the signs. Keep following the flags and the patrol.

Know what you’re reading

Two readings.
Two different jobs.

01

The beach risk score

A number out of 100 on the beach card, accompanied by a risk label and conditions. It is separate from any rip outline.

02

Detection confidence

A percentage alongside an outline on the Detect screen. It reflects the model’s estimate for that possible rip, not whether swimming is safe.

Neither reading replaces the red and yellow flags or advice from the patrol.

Understanding the limits

A useful view.
Never the whole ocean.

A camera can only show so much. These are the situations where detection may be unavailable, incomplete or wrong.

VISIBILITY

When the image breaks down

Darkness, heavy rain and strong glare can make detection unavailable. Conditions may still be shown.

CAMERA ANGLE

What sits beyond the view

Small, distant or oblique rips can be missed. A camera only covers the part of the water it can see.

FALSE POSITIVES

An outline can be wrong

Shadows, weed and a lull between sets may be mistaken for a rip. A confidence figure does not remove that uncertainty.

CHANGING WATER

One moment is not the next

Rips can form, shift and fade as conditions change. An outline relates to the image being analysed.

A little more detail

Good questions.
Straight answers.

What detection means, where it works and what to keep in mind.

How accurate is it?

The prototype can miss rips and produce false outlines. A confidence percentage is a model reading for one outline, not an independently measured accuracy rate. A published accuracy figure is not available on this site yet. See the pilot’s current scope.

Where has detection been tested?

At Palm Beach. Other beaches may have conditions and a streaming camera, but a Live badge does not mean detection has been tested there. Each new camera needs its own testing. Explore coverage.

What if the camera cannot see clearly?

Detection may be unavailable or unreliable in darkness, rain or glare. The app can still display conditions. Missing detection is never an all-clear.

Does the app need my location?

Location is optional in the app. Allow it to find nearby beaches, or choose a beach yourself. Read about privacy.

Can RipCo tell me it is safe to swim?

No. Use it for additional awareness. Swim between the red and yellow flags and follow the lifesavers and lifeguards on duty. Our safety commitment.

A few terms, explained
Rip current
Water flowing away from shore through the surf zone. Rips vary in shape and behaviour.
Feeders, neck and head
In a common sandbar rip, feeder currents gather water near shore, the neck carries it seaward, and the head spreads beyond the breakers.
Frame
One still image from the camera feed.
Confidence
The model’s estimate for a particular outline. It is not the probability that swimming is safe.
False outline / missed rip
An outline around something that is not a rip / a rip that the model does not outline.
Risk score
A separate number out of 100 on a beach card, labelled as a level of risk.
Live
The camera is streaming. This label does not confirm detection coverage.
Sets and lulls
Groups of waves and the quieter intervals between them.
