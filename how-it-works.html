How it works

From a public camera to a warning on your phone

RipCo does not need new cameras. It reads a feed that already watches the water, runs a model over each frame and shows a likely rip as a red outline on the live view. This page goes through each stage, then through what the model can’t see.

The three stages

  1. It uses a camera that already exists

    Public beach cameras already stream the surf so people can check it from home. RipCo connects to one of those feeds. Nothing new has to be mounted, powered or maintained, which keeps the cost of adding another beach down to software.

  2. A computer-vision model reads each frame

    The model looks at the surface of the water for the signature of a rip: a darker lane cutting through the surf, a gap in the line of breaking waves, and foam or sand streaming out to sea. It has been developed on footage from a public camera at Manly Beach.

  3. A likely rip is outlined on the live view

    When the model finds that signature it draws a red outline on the live image and shows its confidence as a percentage. There is no green state. If the model finds nothing, the app says nothing was found, which is different from saying the water is clear.

What the model looks for

A rip seen from above, and from the beach

Waves push water over the sandbars towards the beach. That water has to get back out, and it does so through the lowest point between the bars. That return flow is the rip.

A · Feeders
Water pushed over the sandbars flows along the beach from both sides towards the low point between them.
B · Neck
The flow turns and runs out through the gap. This is the fastest part: narrow, deeper and darker, with no waves breaking in it.
C · Head
Beyond the breaking waves the current slows and spreads out. Swimmers carried here can move sideways and swim back in.

The neck is the part a beach camera can see, and the part the model is trained on. Three cues give it away.

Cue 1

A darker channel

Deeper water cutting out through the surf reflects less light, so a rip often reads as a darker lane running away from the beach.

Cue 2

A gap in the breaking waves

Waves break on the sandbars either side of a rip but not in the channel itself, leaving a calmer-looking gap in the line of white water.

Cue 3

Foam and sand moving out

Foam, sand and debris streaming away from shore show which way the water is pulling, which separates a rip from a patch of still water.

Limits

What it can’t see

A confidence figure is a reading from a camera. These are the conditions where that reading is weak or missing, and the app is built to say so rather than guess.

  1. Night, heavy rain and strong glare

    The camera image stops being usable. The app shows detection as unavailable, and the conditions data stays.

  2. Small, distant or oblique rips

    A rip that is small, far from the camera or at a shallow angle to it can be missed. A rip can be present without being detected.

  3. False positives

    The model can outline something that is not a rip: a shadow, a patch of weed, a lull between sets. The confidence figure exists so you can weigh each outline.

  4. Water that changes in minutes

    Rips form, move and fade with the sets and the tide. A frame is a moment, not a forecast.

  5. Beaches without a usable camera

    Detection needs a public camera with a clear view of the surf zone. Everywhere else the app shows conditions only, and says so.

Whatever the screen shows

Swim between the red and yellow flags and follow the lifeguards on duty. RipCo can miss a rip. They are reading the water in person.

Common questions

Accuracy, night, coverage and location

How accurate is the detection?

Every outline carries a confidence percentage, and that figure is a reading rather than a promise. The model can miss rips and can outline things that are not rips. Nothing in the app is presented as an all-clear, so treat every session as though a rip could be present.

Does it work at night or in poor visibility?

Detection needs a usable camera image. At night, in heavy rain or in strong glare, the app shows detection as unavailable rather than guessing. Conditions data stays available, and the rule does not change: flags and lifeguards first.

Which beaches have detection?

Manly Beach, where the model has been developed and tested. Detection can extend to any beach with a public camera that gives a clear view of the surf zone. Until then, other beaches show conditions only. The coverage page lists every beach in the app.

Does it use my location?

Only if you allow it, and only to show beaches near you. You can search for any beach without sharing your location.
