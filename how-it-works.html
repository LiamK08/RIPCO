How it works

From the surf.
To your screen.

RipCo does not need new cameras. It reads a feed that already watches the water, runs a model over each frame and shows a likely rip as a red outline on the live view.

The three stages

  1. It uses a camera that already exists

    Public beach cameras already stream the surf so people can check it from home. RipCo connects to one of those feeds, so adding a beach costs software and testing time, not hardware.

  2. A computer-vision model reads each frame

    The model looks at the surface of the water for the signature of a rip: a darker lane through the surf, a gap in the breaking waves, foam moving out to sea. I trained it on footage from a public camera at Palm Beach.

  3. A likely rip is outlined on the live view

    When the model finds that signature it draws a red outline on the live image and shows its confidence as a percentage. The three things the Detect screen can show.

What the model looks for

A rip seen from above, and from the beach

Waves push water over the sandbars towards the beach. That water gets back out through the lowest point between the bars. That return flow is the rip.

A · Feeders
Water pushed over the sandbars flows along the beach towards the low point between them.
B · Neck
The flow turns and runs out through the gap: narrow, deeper and darker, with no waves breaking in it. The part a camera can see.
C · Head
Beyond the breaking waves the current slows and spreads out.

The model looks for three cues in the neck: a darker channel, a gap in the line of breaking waves, and foam or sand moving out to sea. They are the same cues Surf Life Saving teaches people to look for.

Limits

What it can’t see

In each of these the app marks detection as unavailable, or an outline may be missing or wrong. Swim between the flags whatever the screen shows.

  1. Night, heavy rain and strong glare

    The camera image stops being usable, so the app marks detection as unavailable and keeps showing the conditions.

  2. Small, distant or oblique rips

    A rip that is small, far from the camera or at a shallow angle to it can be missed.

  3. False outlines

    The model can outline a shadow, a patch of weed or a lull between sets. The confidence figure is there so you can weigh each outline.

  4. Water that changes in minutes

    Rips form, move and fade with the sets and the tide, so an outline describes the frame it was drawn on.

The risk score

How the number on each beach card is built

Every beach card carries a score out of 100 with a label beside it, built from the live conditions for that beach. On the home screen shown on this site, the Palm Beach North card reads 55 and Moderate risk in amber, with water at 18.0 °C, air at 13.4 °C, wind at 19 km/h and a 1.2 m swell. The band below Moderate is still a level of risk; the word safe does not appear.

Common questions

Accuracy, night, coverage, location, terms

How accurate is the detection?

Each outline carries a confidence percentage. The model misses some rips and outlines some things that are not rips, and the app never shows an all-clear, so assume a rip could be present in any session. The Palm Beach pilot section is where the test record lives.

Does it work at night or in poor visibility?

Detection needs a usable camera image. At night, in heavy rain or in strong glare the app marks detection as unavailable and keeps showing the conditions. The rule on the sand does not change: flags first.

Which beaches have detection?

Palm Beach, where I trained and tested the model. Detection can extend to any beach with a public camera that gives a clear view of the surf zone. The coverage page lists the beaches this site tracks.

Does it use my location?

Location is optional. If you allow it, the app shows the beaches near you; without it, you can pick beaches from the map. More on the privacy page.

Glossary
Rip current
A narrow, fast channel of water flowing out through the surf, formed where water pushed over the sandbars returns to sea.
Feeders, neck, head
The flows along the beach that gather water (A), the narrow fast part running out through the gap (B), and where the current spreads beyond the waves (C).
Sets and lulls
Waves arrive in groups with calmer gaps between them. A lull can look like a rip for a moment.
Frame
One still image from the camera feed.
Confidence
The model’s own estimate, as a percentage, that an outline is a rip.
False outline, missed rip
An outline on something that is not a rip; a rip present with no outline.
Risk score
The number out of 100 on each beach card, built from the live conditions and labelled as a level of risk.
Live
The tag meaning the camera at that beach is streaming. It does not mean detection has been tested there.
The flags
The red and yellow flags mark the area lifesavers and lifeguards are watching on a patrolled beach.
