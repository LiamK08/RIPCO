Features

What’s in the app

Rip detection is the reason the app exists. Around it sit the conditions, a risk score, alerts, live cameras and two community tools, so you can check one beach in one place before you leave the house. The home screen is annotated below.

01 · The core feature

Live camera with rip detection

The live view from your beach camera, with likely rips outlined in red and a confidence figure beside each outline. You can see where the model is looking and how sure it is, then check that against the water in front of you and against the flags.

I trained and tested the model on a public camera at Manly Beach. Other beaches with a public camera stream live in the app without detection.

Tested at Manly BeachPrototype

What the screen shows
Red outline
Where the model sees the signature of a rip in the current frame.
Confidence
How sure the model is, as a percentage, to weigh against what you can see.
Unavailable
At night, in heavy rain or strong glare the camera image is not usable, and the app says detection is unavailable.
No outline
A frame with no outline means the model found no rip signature in that frame. It is a reading of one moment.
Three things the Detect screen can show

Streaming, outlined, or unavailable

The same camera view in three conditions. Only one of them draws anything on the water. Drawn illustrations; the on-screen wording in the app may differ.

  1. Panel one:

    Streaming, no outline

    The live feed with the model running. No outline means no rip signature in this frame, and the flags line stays under the card.

  2. Panel two:

    Likely rip outlined

    The model found the signature of a rip. It draws the outline in red and shows its confidence as a percentage.

  3. Panel three:

    Detection unavailable

    Night, heavy rain or strong glare: the image is not usable, so the app says detection is unavailable and keeps the conditions.

The home screen, annotated

What each part of the screen is for

The first screen you see when you open the app. It answers one question: what is the water doing at my beach?

1 2 3 4 5 6
  1. Weather now. Air temperature and sky at your location, first, so you can decide before you leave.
  2. Near you. How many beaches are within reach and how many have a camera streaming live, with one row of conditions across them.
  3. Patrol line. Patrol status where it is known, and “Patrol unknown” where it is not. The instruction is the same either way: check the flags.
  4. Live tag. The camera at this beach is streaming. Rip detection itself has only been developed and tested at Manly.
  5. Risk score. A number out of 100 built from the live conditions, with a label beside it; 55 reads Moderate risk in amber. How it is built.
  6. Detect. The live camera view with the rip outline, one tap from any screen. Map, Alerts and Profile sit beside it.
02 to 07

The tools around it

Each one describes the beach. The risk score summarises the conditions as a level of risk, and none of the tools gives a verdict on whether to swim.

02

Safety brief

A short plain-English summary of what the water is doing at your beach right now: the wind, the swell, the temperature and what they add up to, with the reminder to swim between the flags built in.

03

Conditions and risk score

Water and air temperature, wind and swell for your beach, in one view. The app builds a risk score out of 100 from those conditions and shows it as a level of risk, amber for moderate, above the patrol line on each beach card. How the score is built.

04

Alerts

The app watches the beaches you save and flags changing conditions before you head out, so you can decide at home whether to go.

05

Live cameras

Beaches with a public camera stream live in the app and carry a Live tag. Rip detection runs on top of the stream only where I have tested it, which today means Manly.

06

Shark Watch

Hazard sightings reported by other people at the beach, shared with everyone nearby. Reports come from beachgoers and are unverified. If lifeguards close the beach, their call overrides anything in the app.

07

Ask RipCo

A conversational assistant for conditions and beach-safety questions, such as what the surf is doing or what a flag means. It is built to point you to the flags and the lifesavers rather than give a verdict.

Left out on purpose

What isn’t in the app

  • The word “safe”. Each card shows a level of risk instead, and the band below Moderate is still a level of risk.
  • A verdict on whether to swim. The flags and the lifeguards decide that, and every beach card says so.
  • Detection at beaches where I have not tested it. Those beaches show conditions, and a live stream where there is a camera.

The reasoning is on the design decisions section of the About page.
