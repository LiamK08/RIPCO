Features

What’s in the app

Five tools. Rip detection is the reason the app exists. The other four sit beside it so you can check one beach in one place before you leave the house.

01 · The core feature

Live camera with rip detection

The live view from your beach camera, with likely rips outlined in red and a confidence figure beside each outline. You can see where the model is looking and how sure it is, then check that against the water in front of you and against the flags.

Detection has been developed and tested on a public camera at Manly Beach. Every other beach in the app shows conditions only, and the screen says so.

Tested at Manly BeachPrototype

What the screen shows
Red outline
Where the model sees the signature of a rip in the current frame.
Confidence
How sure the model is, as a percentage. A reading from a camera, weighed against what you can see.
Unavailable
At night, in heavy rain or strong glare the app says detection is unavailable instead of guessing.
No green state
The app never marks water as safe. Nothing found means nothing found.
02 to 05

The tools around it

Each one describes the beach. None of them rates it. The app has no screen that tells you whether to swim.

02

Safety brief

A short plain-English summary of what the water is doing at your beach right now: the wind, the swell, the temperature and what they add up to, with the reminder to swim between the flags built in. It describes conditions and never rates them safe.

03

Marine conditions

Wind speed and direction, swell height and period, tide and water temperature for your beach in one view, so you know what to expect before you arrive.

04

Shark Watch

Hazard sightings reported by other people at the beach, shared with everyone nearby. Reports come from beachgoers and are unverified. If lifeguards close the beach, their call overrides anything in the app.

05

Ask RipCo

A conversational assistant for conditions and beach-safety questions: what the surf is doing, what a flag means, how to spot a rip. Its answers point you back to the flags and the lifeguards, and it will not tell you whether to swim.

Left out on purpose

What isn’t in the app

  • A “safe to swim” rating, a green tick or any colour that reads as permission.
  • A swim or don’t-swim verdict from the assistant, the brief or the conditions screen.
  • Detection at beaches where it has not been developed and tested. Those beaches show conditions only.

The reasoning is on the design decisions section of the About page.
