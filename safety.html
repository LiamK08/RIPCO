Safety

The flags first. Then the app.

RipCo helps you read the water before you swim. That only works if the app is clear about what it can and can’t do. This page is that commitment, and it is the same in the app as it is here.

The commitment

What RipCo will and will not do

A structured interview with a Surf Life Saving NSW patrol member at Manly shaped these rules. The commitment is mine. Nothing here is endorsed by Surf Life Saving.

  1. RipCo will never tell you a beach is “safe”

    There is no safe verdict anywhere in the app. It shows what it detects and what the conditions are. It never tells you the water is fine to enter, because an app cannot make that call for you.

  2. It adds to your judgement

    RipCo adds to what you can see with your own eyes and what the lifeguards tell you. Use it alongside both, never instead of either.

  3. Rip currents can appear in calmer-looking water

    A rip can form where the water looks smoothest, and the calmest gap between the waves is often where one is running. A calmer surface is never a sign that the water is fine.

  4. Detection can miss a rip, and can flag one that isn’t there

    A confidence percentage is a reading from a camera. Treat every session as though a rip could be present, whatever the screen shows.

  5. Always swim between the red and yellow flags

    The flags mark the patrolled zone, chosen by lifeguards reading the water in person. If there are no flags and no patrol, do not swim.

  6. Follow the lifeguards, every time

    If a lifeguard’s instruction differs from anything RipCo shows, follow the lifeguard. They are watching the water in person and they have the final word.

Always swim between the red and yellow flags, and follow the lifeguards.

If it happens

If you’re caught in a rip

This is the advice Surf Life Saving Australia gives. It works whether or not you have your phone with you.

  1. Stay calm and float

    A rip pulls you away from shore, not under the water. Conserve your energy.

  2. Don’t swim against it

    Strong swimmers tire fast fighting a current head-on.

  3. Swim parallel to the beach

    Move sideways until you are out of the channel, then angle back towards shore.

  4. Can’t get out? Raise an arm

    Keep floating, raise one arm and call for help.

Source: Surf Life Saving Australia, beachsafe.org.au.
