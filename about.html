Where it started

A beach day starts
before the beach.

RipCo is my Year 12 Design and Technology major work: a free iPhone app designed to make rip current awareness part of getting ready for the beach.

Public cameras already look out over the surf. The idea is to use that view to outline likely rip currents, and put it alongside the conditions on the phone you already carry.

The need behind the idea

Rip currents are Australia’s number one coastal hazard. Surf Life Saving Australia’s National Coastal Safety Report 2025 counted 154 coastal drowning deaths in 2024–25, the highest number on record, with a rip involved in more than one in three beach drowning deaths.1 Its 2018 survey found that three in four people could not spot a rip from the beach.2 Between the flags, lifesavers and lifeguards read the water for you; away from them, you are on your own judgement.

The tools that exist each do part of the job. BeachSafe shows patrol times and hazards, surf cams show live video, forecast apps show swell and wind. None of them reads the live picture for a rip. Many beaches already have a public camera pointed at the surf, and RipCo reads those feeds.

The user is a beachgoer who checks conditions before leaving, which 69% of the people I surveyed already do. They open the app at home in the morning with one question, what is the water doing at my beach, and may look again on the sand with one hand free. So the headline has to be readable in one glance, and the detection view is one tap from any screen.

Interviews with patrol members shaped the safety commitment: no “safe” verdict anywhere in the app, and the reminder to check the flags on each beach card. The views are mine; nothing here is endorsed by Surf Life Saving.

Process

From an idea
to something you can hold.

Six stages, from a hazard most people cannot see to a prototype running on a real camera.

  1. Identify the need

    Rips are the number one hazard on Australian beaches, and most people cannot pick one from the sand.

  2. Research

    A survey of beachgoers, interviews with patrol members, and the national coastal safety reports.

  3. Concept

    Use the public cameras already pointed at the surf, on the phone people already carry.

  4. Prototype

    An iPhone app with live cameras, conditions, a risk score and alerts, and a model trained on footage from Palm Beach.

  5. Test

    The model run on the Palm Beach camera and its outlines checked against the water, with the limits recorded as they appeared.

  6. Evaluate and refine

    The prototype judged against the criteria below; next, the early-access list, App Review and a second beach.

Made with intention

Every detail
has a reason.

Six choices shaped by research, patrol-member interviews and testing the prototype at Palm Beach.

Awareness in your pocket.
Judgement on the beach.

01

Use cameras that already exist

New hardware would need money, permission and maintenance at every beach. Public cameras already look at the surf, so adding a beach costs software and testing. Nothing is manufactured, mounted or powered.

From: the need, and the budget of a student project.

02

Show an outline and a confidence figure

A tick or a cross would ask you to trust the model. An outline shows where it is looking and the percentage shows how sure it is, so you can check both against what you see.

From: the survey, where 78% could not pick a rip and 84% wanted it shown on the camera.

03

A risk score instead of a safety rating

Each beach card carries a score out of 100 labelled as a level of risk. The lowest band is still a level of risk, and the word safe does not appear in the app. How the score is built.

From: the patrol-member interviews.

04

Flags first, under every card

The line “check the flags” sits under each beach card with the patrol status where known, and “Patrol unknown” where not. Missing information never reads as reassurance.

From: the interviews and Surf Life Saving guidance.

05

Live means the camera, not the model

The Live tag says the camera is streaming. Detection is shown separately, on the Detect screen, only where I have tested it, so a stream is never mistaken for a checked beach.

From: testing, where most cameras stream and one has been checked.

06

Free, one beach and one platform first

A safety tool behind a paywall protects the people who pay, so there is no subscription. Palm Beach and iPhone first, so I could train and test detection in one place before adding anything else.

From: the survey (84% would use a free mobile tool) and testing.

What I would change

  • Detection has been tested on one camera; the next beach needs its own footage and its own test, and the feed belongs to someone else.
  • iPhone only for now, and live video costs data and battery on the sand.
  • Not yet shown to users beyond the interviews. Showing the build to survey respondents is the next evaluation step.
